# R2 — held arrival, independent hero, separate VR chapter

25 October 2026. User feedback on the first Logo → Lens study. This narrows the authorized revision to the opening study; no complete event-page rebuild is requested yet.

## User decisions and unresolved judgment

- The 1.5-second auto-dismissed logo is too fast. The icon should reveal, be given time, then transition on scroll or another clear entry action.
- The hero deserves its own space, authority and typography entrance. It must not disappear immediately as part of a single shared hero/VR transformation.
- VR should begin as the next chapter. The optical entrance should feel more like an eye opening / entering a game, not merely a rounded rectangular image frame.
- A property interior should support actual playback or scroll-controlled video where feasible, with a deliberate exit.
- The current posed headset-host image is questioned, not accepted as the final hero.
- The user is interested in the effect and feeling, not committed to Blender, a particular library, a native 3D model or a paid video provider.

## Implemented experiment being commissioned

1. Reveal the real mark, hold the assembled mark until an explicit scroll/Enter action, then let its facets transition into the hero. Replay, tap/keyboard entry, static and reduced motion remain available. Normal scrolling must not be intercepted globally.
2. Give the hero independent full-screen presence and a distinct masked/staggered title arrival. Use a revised illustrative gathering scene to test a more event-led atmosphere. Preserve clear event identity, date/place/action.
3. Start a separate VR chapter below the hero. Test a short reversible eye/aperture entrance, distinct foreground/background movement, then a coherent native interior clip with matching poster. Avoid pretending unrelated stills and clips form one continuous room/camera.
4. Test actual media readiness and time progression/seeking, not only CSS or a screenshot. Preserve a readable fallback if video or JavaScript fails.
