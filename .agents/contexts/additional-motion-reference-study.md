## Important disagreements and corrections

- Rexan presents Remotion/HyperFrames as necessary for renderable video. The bespoke browser/FFmpeg workflow in earlier sources and /brag-slim's documented route show that a framework is one option. A rough HTML prototype is not the only framework-free possibility.
- Hamza's title refers to eight repositories, but one entry is a Remotion documentation page. His own text acknowledges it cannot be cloned. Installing Remotion agent instructions also does not install a complete project's runtime dependencies.
- 21st.dev is a component catalog with multiple ways to bring source into a project, not a uniquely specified mandatory package. Good components still need a coherent RAMA layout, genuine content and seek-safe animation.
- Current /brag documentation distinguishes classic HyperFrames-based /brag from /brag-slim without HyperFrames or bundled assets. Do not assume the linked skill still has only the route described in an older post.
- Hand-painted cartoon kits have their own drawing/character conventions. Their no-text or continuous-scene preferences are unsuitable as blanket rules for a product film that needs readable valuation numbers and explanations.
- Using one mathematical time grid helps synchronization, but it does not prove final playback/encoding has no audio drift. Sound must be assessed in the exported film.

## What creating the film actually involves

This is the integrated understanding to carry forward, not an authorized implementation plan:

1. **Establish the communication job.** Audience, viewer question, product evidence, message and action govern the film. Do not start by selecting every impressive effect.
2. **Prepare an asset and product record.** Approved identity, interface/source screenshots, sample-data labels, fonts, imagery and usage rights. Distinguish the proposed RAMA interface from the live app.
3. **Explore a small number of coherent directions.** Show what differs in tone, structure and information density. Reference grammar supplies options, not a compulsory style.
4. **Review representative stills.** Establish composition, type, readable numbers and content before expensive animation. Stills cannot prove pace or transition quality.
5. **Write the timed sequence.** Each scene/state has a purpose, start/end, content, transitions, hold time and sound events. UI must preserve cause/effect. Narration, if chosen, affects timing early.
6. **Choose one rendering route.** React compositions, seekable HTML/SVG/Canvas, or an appropriate drawing kit. Include generated footage only when it serves the story. Runtime and skills are distinct layers.
7. **Implement deterministic motion and assets.** A frame at a requested time should not depend on previous frames, timers, unseeded randomness or unfinished asset loading. Cached geometry/text metrics and stable asset inputs reduce repeated work.
8. **Inspect a short animatic and the costly transitions.** Check reading time, hierarchy, collisions, camera movement, cursor causality and sudden visual jumps. Do not render every possible direction at full resolution first.
9. **Develop and mix sound.** Coordinate selected sound events and music, reserve space for speech, and check peaks and final playback. Procedural audio is optional; a synth or a voice clone is not a prerequisite for quality.
10. **Render and verify.** Inspect consecutive frames around transitions and seek repeatability where relevant, then play the encoded output with sound. Check actual dimensions, duration, format, text safety and start/end behaviour. A contact sheet or self-score alone is insufficient.
11. **Adapt delivery layouts.** Recompose for placement; avoid blind cropping of UI. Track aspect-ratio versions, cutdowns and different concepts as distinct deliverables.
12. **Preserve what worked.** Save source, assets, decisions, timing and repeatable commands. Only then evaluate whether a reusable project template or skill reduces later effort.

Reading the corpus improves conceptual understanding; implementation experience still requires a bounded render test. Do not claim expert-level execution or a working studio based on these readings alone.

## RAMA fit and reference translation

KEEP: explicit product evidence, specific scene states, still-first review, readable typography, deterministic timing and targeted revision notes.

ADAPT: storyboard comparison to a small scope the user can meaningfully review; continuous UI morphing to transitions that preserve property/input/result meaning; beat timing to include sufficient reading holds; textured illustration only where the brand/story warrants it.

REJECT as defaults: mandatory installation of the whole list; source claims that a single library guarantees premium design; another company's proof numbers; a fast component montage that obscures RAMA's value; copying reference characters or branding.

DEFER: voice replication, extensive 3D/characters, provider integrations, repository cloning, plugin installation and a custom skill until their role in a concrete production is established. This is a current scope judgement, not a permanent prohibition.

For a rendered film, check phone-size readability and captions/muted understanding. If an interactive prototype accompanies it, it additionally needs keyboard access, responsive states and reduced-motion behaviour; film-only flourishes should not quietly become mandatory app interactions.

## Voice decision

The user is interested in potentially using their own voice later. That preference is preserved, not a commitment to cloning. An original recording, a chosen narrator, synthetic narration or no narration are still open options. The film should be designed to communicate when muted. A music-and-text teaser can be strong without voice; a more explanatory version may benefit from speech. Decide from the script and audience rather than the existence of a voice tool. Do not seek recordings, call APIs or replicate a voice during this research.
