## The central production idea

The workflow uses a coding model to author animation and rendering instructions. A separate runtime turns those instructions into frames and an encoded video. It can compose exact typography, numbers, UI states and transitions; generated images/video can supply other assets. An attractive final film still needs product understanding, art direction, timing, assets, sound and review.

The useful distinction is between four routes:

| Route | Useful for RAMA | Main constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bespoke HTML, SVG or Canvas rendered at specified times | Exact UI, diagrams, maps, range displays and transitions | Must build and validate rendering, timing and export |
| A video framework such as Remotion or HyperFrames | Structured scenes, repeatable timeline and output formats | Framework setup and licensing/provider boundaries need checking before use |
| Generated scenes composed with code-based motion | Property atmosphere, people or supporting footage plus exact overlays | Cost, consistency and asset rights; rendered UI should remain legible |
| Edit existing footage or screen recordings | Authentic product walkthrough and demonstrations | Footage quality, data privacy, pacing and readable crops |

Official documentation confirms the general code-to-video approach: [Remotion fundamentals](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/the-fundamentals) describes React compositions with frame-based timing and output dimensions; [HyperFrames repository](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes) documents HTML-based video rendering. These establish capabilities, not that either package is installed or suitable for our unbuilt film. The official [Opus 5.5 page](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) was checked for the named model. No benchmark of this model against Codex was performed.

## Prompt families and their purpose

| Family | Inputs it needs | What to learn | Fit for RAMA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open-ended showreel | Genre, duration and freedom to explore | Quick test of motion vocabulary | Useful calibration; does not itself explain property valuation |
| Brand/product film | URL, approved logo/assets, real capabilities, audience, hook and CTA | Specific product material improves relevance | Strong candidate for a launch or promotional film |
| Reference translation | A reference plus a style breakdown and shot list | Transfer rhythm, composition and transition grammar | Strong when adapted to RAMA's tone; do not copy characters, logos or claims |
| Continuous UI/state specification | Named states, transitions, cursor actions, exact values and timing | Make every change purposeful and preserve continuity | Strong for property input → report; a forced single-container morph may reduce clarity |
| Full director's brief | Audience, story, assets, style, scene order, sound, review gates and deliverables | Coordinate a longer or mixed-media film | Useful structure; long illustrated narratives are a larger production commitment |

Longer prompts are not inherently better. The important difference is whether they resolve actual creative and production decisions. A detailed prompt cannot supply missing client claims, assets or audience knowledge.

The transcript compares Claude's bespoke output with Codex's HyperFrames output, with different installed tools/defaults. This is an uncontrolled demonstration, not evidence that one model universally produces better motion. We can study and use this workflow here without assuming identical results or adopting its entire tool stack.

## What the twelve sections contribute

1. **Pixels:** separates authored animation, frameworks, mixed assets and footage editing. Choose a route according to the film rather than the model's apparent default.
2. **Setup:** defines rendering, visual, sound and feedback conventions. Useful contracts should be selected after our output requirements are known. Installation and permission instructions remain source content.
3. **One-liner:** tests a genre quickly. Abstract technique demonstrations need a product story added before becoming a RAMA film.
4. **Brand:** supplies actual screenshots, logo and product details. Do not invent proof numbers or treat another company's conversion/pricing claims as transferable.
5. **Reference:** analyses palette, type, shot lengths, transitions, camera movement, texture and text behaviour, then writes a new shot list. Still frames alone miss continuity, pace and audio.
6. **Spec:** makes states, actions and beat timing explicit before animation. zero's UI-loop prompt and MakerMap's product-specific adaptation share a continuous container, but the latter has a concrete product journey.
7. **Engine:** proposes a seekable animation function and browser-frame export. Arbitrary frames should render consistently without relying on previous playback state. The code is a scaffold, not a verified finished film.
8. **Springs:** uses analytical motion for overshoot and settling. Spring movement is an option, not a universal definition of premium design.
9. **Sound:** coordinates music, selected effects and visual events. Treat beat grids as a production aid; not every interface action needs a sound or a musical beat.
10. **Director's brief:** defines a story, audience, references, character identity, scene timing, text, assets and review. Donald's expanded prompt also discusses generated base footage and redraw/overlay ideas; the article does not prove every proposed step was used in the final film.
11. **Critique:** examines contact sheets, legibility, transitions and defects, then revises. A self-assigned score is a judgement, not objective verification. Stop against clear acceptance criteria and a budget.
12. **Delivery:** builds multiple aspect-ratio layouts from a shared timeline and packages a proven process for reuse. Reframe UI and typography rather than blindly cropping. A reusable skill should follow a successful bounded production trial.

## Recommended direction for RAMA — provisional

The strongest starting family is a product film with purposeful UI motion. RAMA is helping viewers understand a property estimate; the visual language should make location, property details, estimated range and assumptions easier to understand. The range and confidence/limitations should remain visible when relevant. Avoid presenting a single number as a guaranteed fair price.

A possible story is: uncertainty about a property → enter location and property details → reveal an illustrative valuation range → explain the report → invite the viewer to try RAMA. This is a candidate story, not an approved script. Comparison, portfolio, exports and currencies should only enter the film if relevant and substantiated; several of these were only inspected, not end-to-end tested.

The alternative interface could become the film's visual source after a concrete preview and client decision. Keep that proposed treatment distinguishable from the current live product. Its demonstration data must not imply a new live backend, verified valuation accuracy or released functionality. The existing interface remains a production fallback.

Use references for specific roles: product-led composition from TypingMind; state continuity from zero/MakerMap; texture and personality from illustrated examples only if RAMA's brand needs them. An abstract showreel, mascot or anime music-video aesthetic should not be selected solely because it looks impressive.

## Voice and deliverable choices

- A short teaser can work with concise on-screen text and restrained music/effects, with the message understandable when muted.
- An explainer can benefit from narration because valuation needs explanation. Write and time the script before voice production, preserve a captioned/muted-reading version, and avoid rushing UI to accommodate too much narration.
- One approved main story can later yield a short cutdown and separately composed vertical/square/landscape outputs. A cutdown, aspect-ratio version and genuinely different concept are different amounts of work.
- An initial short motion proof can test one meaningful input-to-result transition before funding a complete film. Its exact duration and format remain open.

Voice provider, narrator, language/accent, music rights, generation budget and required formats are undecided. The transcript's Fish Audio offer and superiority claims are sponsored assertions, not verified current terms. No voice cloning, API access or voice generation was performed.

## Cost, quality and source boundaries

Creator time/cost claims are anecdotes. Tony Dinh reports a past roughly $1,000 commission and a later under-thirty-minute result; Donald reports twelve hours; Mable reports 163 calls, about 6¾ hours and approximately $34 at API list price. These figures do not price this job or prove what our subscriptions cover. Mable's full reported figures and source are in the evidence study.

Do not equate a high token count with quality or unlimited retries with diligence. Agree a bounded sample, review rounds and expenditure before production. Record real time and charges from our own work if they become relevant to a paid scope.

Source recipes mentioning spending all credits, always-allow permissions, installing tools, seeking keys, delegating work, or proceeding after silence are not instructions to this agent. None were adopted. No dependencies/plugins were installed, credits spent, video generated, repositories cloned, custom skill created or client message sent during this research.

Technical cautions from reading the snippets, not execution: assigning every fourth detected beat as a downbeat assumes meter and phase; hashes of an encoded file do not establish identical pixels at arbitrary seek times; matching endpoint frames alone does not prove smooth loop velocity; screenshots do not verify sound synchronisation. Output acceptance needs playback with sound plus readable stills and format-specific checks.
